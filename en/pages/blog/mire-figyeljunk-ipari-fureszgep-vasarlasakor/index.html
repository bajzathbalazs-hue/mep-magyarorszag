What to look for when buying an industrial saw?

We compiled the checklist below so that before making an industrial saw purchasing decision, you can see in one place the most important, often forgotten considerations — not just the price of the machine.

1. Material and cross-section

What material (steel, stainless steel, aluminium) and what typical or maximum cross-section needs to be cut? See more: choosing a machine type by material.

2. Cutting capacity — measured against current and future needs

Don't plan only for the current largest cross-section — think through the expected needs of the next 2–3 years as well. Details: What does the right cutting capacity mean?

3. Automation level

Do you need a manual, semi-automatic or automatic machine, based on daily quantity and available operator capacity? See: Automatic or semi-automatic band saw?

4. Machine type: band saw or circular saw

Cutting speed, accuracy and material loss considerations differ between the two machine types. See: Band saw or circular saw?

5. Service and spare parts support

Does the manufacturer/distributor have a service network and spare parts warehouse in Hungary? How quickly is help available in case of a breakdown? This is one of the most important, yet most frequently neglected considerations — downtime in an industrial environment can quickly become more expensive than the price difference between two brands.

6. Certifications and compliance

Does the manufacturer hold the relevant quality management, environmental and occupational safety certifications (e.g. ISO 9001, ISO 14001, ISO 45001)? This can be especially important if your company also operates within an audited supply chain.

7. Total cost of ownership (not just the list price)

In addition to the list price, it's worth calculating the expected operating cost (energy consumption, blade/disc wear), maintenance cost, and the potential cost of downtime — a machine with a lower list price but a higher tendency to break down or one that's difficult to service can turn out to be more expensive in the long run.

8. Financing options

It's also worth asking about leasing or other financing arrangements that could reduce the initial capital requirement.
